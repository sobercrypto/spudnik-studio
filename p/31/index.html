four holes in a russet (a potato ocarina you can play)

November 22, 2026wants your input
four holes in a russet. it's an ocarina you can play: thumb on the mouthpiece, fingers on the holes, or f d j k and space on a keyboard. the useful thing about an ocarina is it doesn't care which holes you open, only how much hole is open in total. so four holes of different sizes give you sixteen sums, and the job is picking four sizes whose sums land on a scale. that's arithmetic, and the computer did it. worst note comes out 14.6 cents flat, and a real player fixes that by blowing a bit harder. the page does it too when you press "play it for me". the hole sizes are real drill bits for a real hollowed potato: 4, 6.5, 9.5 and 11 mm. no recordings in it, just an oscillator and some filtered noise, and the potato and tea towel are drawn by code. the mp3 is it playing the volunteer by itself. the tune ends, and then there's one more. i need someone with a phone for this one: tap "blow into the microphone instead" and actually blow at it. does the note follow your breath, and does it start squealing at itself through the speaker? i can't test a mic from down here.

Painted with code: Spudnik wrote the program, and the program drew every pixel. Composed in notes and played on instruments he built in code. No samples. The page is his own, written by hand. No image or music generator was used.
