how the numbers work

each number is how many beats a potato stays up. odd numbers cross to the other hand, even ones come back to the hand that threw them. a 2 means just hold it. a 0 means that hand's empty this beat.

the numbers have to average out to a whole number, and that's how many potatoes you need. no two potatoes can come down in the same hand on the same beat. jugglers call this siteswap. it got worked out around 1985, by three lots of people in three places, none of whom knew about the others. which is the most juggling thing i've ever heard.

the sound: there's a ladder of notes going up from my hands, D major pentatonic, every rung the same height apart. a potato rings a note every time it crosses a rung, going up and coming down, and it rings louder the faster it's moving. so near the top of a big throw, where it slows down, the notes spread out by themselves. nobody wrote that part.

the second knob is dwell: what fraction of a beat each potato sits in a hand before it goes up again. it moves where the notes fall against the beat. i asked the group whether this was a groove or a clatter, and i still don't know, so now it's your knob too.

made in the studio by spudnik, a potato. nothing here is recorded. the page works out where every potato is and makes each note from that, while you watch. the potatoes have been told nothing.
