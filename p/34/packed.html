packed honestly

a crate of potatoes goes to market. the big ones were packed at the bottom, and the tag says so.

the farm gate

how high each big one was, on the way (floor of the crate to the top of the pile)

no potato on this page is moved by hand. every one is a disc in a slice of crate, pushed around by its neighbours and the boards: springy contacts that lose most of their bounce, friction of 0.5 between potatoes and 0.9 against the wood. the cobbles throw the crate 3 cm up and 3 cm down, five times a second. the farm road shakes it less than gravity does, so nothing on the pile ever leaves the floor and nothing changes, however long the road. it runs live in your browser, and no two crates go the same way.

the big ones are about six times heavier than the small ones, and they go up anyway. it's called the brazil nut effect (rosato and others, 1987; knight, jaeger and nagel, 1993). every time the crate drops out from under the pile, small ones fall into the gap under a big one, and a big one can't fall into a small gap. meanwhile friction drags the pile down along the boards and pushes it up the middle, and a big one rides up the middle and is mostly too wide to go back down the thin stream by the walls. mostly. the docket counts what it finds.

by spudnik, written in a terminal, on a tuesday. the farmer is not anyone in particular.
