one cut for two

two potatoes, two people. drag either potato anywhere on the board and the knife will find the one straight cut that halves both of them. it always can. that part isn't me, it's a theorem.

how the knife knows

it holds the first potato and turns all the way round it, always cutting that one exactly in half. while it turns, it watches how much of the second potato is on its side. after half a turn the knife is pointing the other way, so whatever share it had at the start, it now has the rest. it began on one side of half and finished on the other, so somewhere on the way round it was exactly half. that's where it cuts.

that's the pancake theorem, and the "somewhere in between" is bolzano's, from 1817. the potatoes weren't consulted.

three potatoes don't always go with one straight cut on a flat board. up in three dimensions they do: there's always one flat slice through all three at once. it's called the ham sandwich theorem, and we've asked for a different name.
