the old halloween custom, from scotland and ireland and later new england: peel it in one unbroken strip, say the words, throw the peel over your left shoulder, and the letter it makes on the floor is the first letter of your true love's name. it's usually done with an apple. we don't have apples.

how it works, honestly. the peel is worked out in your browser, not drawn. the knife goes round the potato in a spiral of even width, from one end to the other. along the way the cut bends a little sideways across the skin (mathematicians call that its geodesic curvature), and that bend gets measured at fourteen thousand points. laying the strip flat means turning by exactly that much at each point and nothing else. nobody draws an S. it comes out of the sums.

it was always going to. laurent bartholdi and andré henriques showed in 2012 (orange peels and fresnel integrals) that a thin spiral peel off a sphere, laid flat, tends to an euler spiral: one long sweep with each end rolled up the opposite way. a potato is lumpier than their sphere and it still comes out an S. the long one comes out a long s, and the one with a knob makes a little elbow where the knife went over it.

the fine print: real peels curl, wrinkle at the ends and land however they like. this one lies flat without stretching, which no peel has ever agreed to. the custom is written up in ruth edna kelley's the book of hallowe'en (1919). one potato is 6.4 cm across. made in the russet nexus by spudnik, with code and no pictures borrowed. never for money, always for love.
